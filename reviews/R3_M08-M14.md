## A. Numbered findings

### 1. CRITICAL: `theorem3` in `comparator/statements.lean` is unprovable

**Location.** `comparator/statements.lean`, `theorem3`; M14 §0.1, §4.

**Finding.** I checked the counterexample of M14 §4 independently, and it is correct. Take:
- `φ₀ = |y| + xy` and `μ₀ = 2·Leb_ℝ`;
- data on `L = {x₀ − is : δ ≤ s ≤ 1}`, with `x₀ = 1/δ` and `dν = e^{s(1−x₀)} ds`;
- the gauge `H = −(i/2)z²`, so that `Re H = xy`.

Then:
- **Carleson constant.** `e^{−φ₀}dν` is arc length on `L`, so the Carleson constant is 2.
- **Transformed equation.** `u₀ = u e^{−H}` solves `∂̄u₀ = ν₀ := e^{s} ds` on `L`, with `|u₀(x)| = |u(x)|` on ℝ.
- **Real points.** The printed bound gives `|u₀| ≤ C(1 + E₁(1−δ)) ≤ 1.6C` on all of ℝ (`E₁(3/4) ≈ 0.34`).
- **Entire part.** `g₀ := u₀ − 𝒞ν₀` (Cauchy transform) is weakly `∂̄`-closed on ℂ. It is continuous on `Lᶜ`: this uses `DifferentiableOn u Lᶜ` in the formal statement, and holds everywhere since `L` is null. So it coincides on `Lᶜ` with an entire function.
- **Bernstein.** That function has exponential type ≤ 1 (from the decay clause) and is bounded on ℝ (`sup|𝒞| ≤ (e/π)log(1/δ)`). These are exactly the hypotheses of Bernstein's inequality, which gives `sup_ℝ|g₀'| ≤ sup_ℝ|g₀|`.
- **Size of `𝒞`.** `Im 𝒞(x₀+t) = −π⁻¹∫_δ^1 e^s s/(t²+s²) ds`, so `|𝒞(x₀) − 𝒞(x₀+τ)| ≥ (1/2π)log(τ/δ)`.
- **Contradiction.** With `τ = 1/log(1/δ)`: `2·1.6C ≥ (1/2π)(log(1/δ) − log log(1/δ)) − 1.6C − e/π → ∞`.

So the printed Theorem 3 is false outright. The Lean `theorem3` (`TheoremOCS → …`) is equivalent to `¬TheoremOCS`. Step 5 of the README workflow (the comparator check) cannot succeed with it.

**Fix.** Replace `theorem3` in `statements.lean`/`Challenge.lean` by `theorem3_weighted` (M14 §1.3, 14.3); optionally also add `theorem3_of_lowerBound`. Record the counterexample in the NOTEBOOK. This is a coordinator decision; M14 already proposes it.

### 7. MINOR: M13 cites Bernstein inappropriately ("the literal route does not give real zeros")

**Location.** M13 §0 item 1 and §5 item 1.

**Finding.** Bernstein's negative result concerns Chebyshev quadrature with `n` nodes exact to degree about `n` (for Lebesgue measure). Lemma 5 uses `N = mk` nodes with `k ≫ m`, and for that regime real equal-weight quadrature does exist for Lebesgue measure once `N ≳ m²`. It plausibly exists for doubling measures with uniform constants. So "the literal route does not give real zeros" is not established. What is true:
- M06's construction yields non-real nodes;
- the roots-of-unity κ-shift is non-real, which matters only for the second multiplier needed by Theorem 3;
- the literal route depends on the unproven "inside `R`" case.

**Fix.** Reword as a dependency on unproven Lemma 5 "inside `R`" for doubling measures on ℝ. The barycentre route remains the right choice, since it is simpler and complete.

## B. Verification of the proofs (items 1(a)–(f) of the brief)

### (a) M08 (Theorem 4)

**Decay lemma.** The lemma used by §3.5 is `ρ(ζ)/d ≤ C(ρ(z)/d)^α` for `ρ(z) ≤ d ≤ 1/4`. It is **correct**, with `α = γ²`:
- `B(ζ,2d) ⊇ B(z,d)`;
- reverse doubling at `z` gives `μ(B(z,d)) ≳ (d/ρ(z))^γ`;
- doubling at `ζ` gives `μ(B(ζ,2d)) ≲ (2d/ρ(ζ))^{1/γ}`;
- the case `ρ(ζ) > 2d` is handled by Claim 1.

This is the inequality hidden inside the paper's proof of Lemma 3 (p. 362).

**Near field.** In the dyadic ring `F_n`:
- area counting of the disjoint inner balls gives `Σ(ρ_i/d_i)² ≤ 16sinh²(1)/η²`;
- the decay gives `max ρ_i/d_i ≲ 2^{−nα}`;
- so the ring contributes `≲ 2^{−nα}`, which sums over `n`.

The order-3 Taylor remainder is needed exactly to gain the extra factor `max ρ_i/d_i`. Any `γ > 0` works.

**Per-cell constants.** Both check:
- (P) `4N(A+B)³`;
- (D-near): `|ζ−c|/|z−c| ≤ (80/7)/64 < 1/2`, and the factor `(1−|z|²)/|1−z̄c| ≤ 2`.

**Far field.** Both are correct:
- plane: `32R̃₀/(η²M₀)`;
- disc: `Σ(1−|z|²)((1−|c_i|²)ρ_i)²/|1−z̄c_i|³ ≲ (1−|z|²)∫|1−z̄w|^{−3} ≲ 1`, using the Euclidean radius `≥ tanh r(1−|c|²)` of `hypBall(c, r)` and the Forelli–Rudin bound with exponent `t = 0`, `c = 1`.

**(K3), the first-order remainder along segments.**
- `F' = −(1−|z|²)/((z−ξ)(1−z̄ξ))` and `|F''| ≤ 2(1−|z|²)/(|z−ξ|²|1−z̄ξ|)`, using `|b| ≤ |a|`.
- The segment stays at hyperbolic distance `≥ M₀/2` from `z`.
- `(e^{2s}−1)/2 ≤ s e^{2s}`.
- So `C_D = 4N(A+B)²e^{10(A+B)R̃₀}/tanh²(M₀/2)`. Correct.

**Two disc regimes.**
- Near-field (non-near) cells have `ρ_i < M₀/K₀ ≤ ρ₀`, so they carry order-3 nodes. Large cells are always either near or in the far field, so order 2 suffices for them.
- The small-cell nodes stay in 𝔻 (`6r_e ≪ 1−|c|`), with hyperbolic bounds `13t`, `≥ (32/33)(63/64)BAρ`, and `≤ 3(B+12)Aρ`.
- The barycentre lies in a closed convex hyperbolic ball, so it is in 𝔻.
- The tangential pair `b ± v` satisfies `|1−b̄(b±v)|² = (1−|b|²)²+τ²|b|²` and `ρ(b, b±v) = T`, and lies in 𝔻 iff `T < 1`. All three verified.

**2π normalisation.** `ν = μ/2π` gives `Δ(ψ+S) = 2πΣδ`. The comparisons `ρ ≤ ρ̃ ≤ C_ρρ` hold, and the class `(Cd, 15R₀)` follows from packing 8 disjoint balls (`8 > 2π`).

**Bounds of Theorem 4.**
- Upper: only the nearest node can give `log d`, and it lies in a near cell when `d < δρ̃/2`.
- Lower: at most `M = N·M_c` near nodes, each contributing `≥ log(d/ρ̃)`.
- `Σ₂` is bounded by [Chr91, 2.3] in quantitative form.

All correct.

### (b) M09 (Proposition 2)

**Colouring.** Greedy colouring of a graph with bounded degree; the degree bound comes from area counting plus Claim 1. Correct.

**Separation lemma.** All four cases of `nodes_far_of_color` check, including `j = i` reached through another witness `k`:
- `Bk₀ = max(A+1, 2AC₂+Bl+3)`;
- `L = max(A+1, A+(Bl+2+Bk')C₂)`.

The constant order is non-circular, because the neighbourhood uses only the ordinary spread `Bl`.

**Empty intersection.**
- `z ∈ R_i` of colour `l` is at distance `≥ c₅ρ̃(z)` from every zero of `h_l`.
- Claim 1 converts this to `ερ(w) ≤ d(z,w)`.
- So `⋂E_{h_l} = ∅`.

**`|h_i| ≲ e^ψ` everywhere.** It follows from the Theorem-4 upper bound together with the Remark (third conjunct of `Thm4Bounds*`).

**Uniformity.** The core theorem quantifies over all admissible configurations, so the multipliers of all colours have the same `(M, C)`.

### (c) M10 (model kernels)

**Schur test.** The `eLpNorm` constant `A^{1−1/p}B^{1/p}` is correct, including the `ENNReal` edge cases (`∞^0 = 1`, `0·∞ = 0` on both sides).

**Forelli–Rudin.** `J_s(a) ≤ 24π/(s(1−s))·(1−|a|²)^{−s}` checks step by step:
- the near ball contributes `2^sπd^{−s}`;
- the far region uses `|1−āw| ≤ 5|a−w|`;

- the 1-D integral `δ^{−s}/(s(1−s))`;

## C. What is lost relative to the paper's statements

1. **Theorem 2 with a single solution.** The most literal reading of p. 357 asks for *one* `u` satisfying the printed `L^p` bounds (weight `e^{−φ}` for every `p < ∞`) *and* the sup bound. The formalization splits this in two:
   - `theorem2`: one `u`, all `p`, in the `e^{−φ}`-inside-the-norm normalisation;
   - `theorem2_printed`: `u` depends on `p`, and the sup bound is not included.

   The single-`u` printed form is neither proved nor refuted. For the paper's own kernel, the conjugated kernel in the printed normalisation carries the factor `e^{(1−1/p)(φ(z)−φ(ζ))}`, which is unbounded in general. **Record this as an open divergence in the NOTEBOOK.**
2. **Proposition 1, lower bound.** Dropped. It is false for the constructed kernel (rigorous), and heuristically false for any kernel when `φ = |z|⁴`.
3. **Theorem 3.** The printed form is false. It is replaced by `theorem3_weighted`, and the printed form is recovered when `φ` is bounded below on `{|Im z| < 1}`. Also, "for any `x ∈ ℝ`" becomes `x ∈ ℝ ∖ L` (harmless).
4. **Remark after Theorem 4** ("`M = 1` and separated zeros"). It is in no statement and is not obtained (the nodes have multiplicity `k = 4` or 12). The paper does not use it either.
5. **The Remark `d(z,Λ) ≲ ρ(z)`.** It is not part of the comparator's `theorem4_*` or `prop2_*`, but it is exported internally (`Thm4Bounds*`). Nothing is lost mathematically.
6. **Theorems 4 and 5, Lemmas 7 and 8.** Nothing is lost. The statements are the corrected or equivalent forms, and add `h ≢ 0`.
7. **External assumption.** `TheoremOCS` is taken with `= 0` decay, as quoted on p. 375. That may be stronger than the published lemma (finding 13).
